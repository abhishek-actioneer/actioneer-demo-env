# Aptus Value Housing Finance FY24: Growth, Profitability, and Expansion Insights

## Executive Summary

Aptus Value Housing Finance delivered exceptional double-digit growth across its assets, earnings, and operational reach in FY24, solidifying its position in the affordable housing finance sector. The company's Assets Under Management (AUM) surged to ₹8,722 crores, representing a robust 29% year-on-year increase [1]. This growth was fueled by a 31% jump in loan disbursements, which reached ₹3,127 crores [1]. Profitability mirrored this expansion, with Profit After Tax (PAT) climbing 22% to ₹612 crores on the back of ₹1,417 crores in total income [1]. 

Crucially, Aptus achieved this rapid expansion while improving its asset quality. Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA) improved to 1.07%, and Net Non-Performing Assets (NNPA) fell to 0.80% [1]. The company maintains a formidable capital buffer, with a Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) of 73.03%, well above regulatory requirements [1]. With the addition of 31 new branches and a 25% increase in its customer base to 133,499, Aptus has established a strong platform for sustained, profitable growth in underserved Tier-2, Tier-3, and Tier-4 markets [1].

## Financial Performance

Aptus demonstrated strong top-line and bottom-line momentum in FY24, driven by efficient operations and a broadening customer base. Total income grew by 25% to ₹1,417 crores, up from ₹1,134 crores in FY23 [1]. This revenue expansion was primarily supported by a 25% increase in interest income, which reached ₹1,320 crores, alongside fee and other income growth from ₹75 crores to ₹97 crores [1]. 

The company's profitability metrics remain highly attractive. Profit Before Tax (PBT) increased by 21% to ₹793 crores, while PAT grew by 22% to ₹612 crores [1]. This earnings growth translated into strong shareholder returns, with Return on Equity (ROE) improving to 17.25% from 16.34% in the previous year, and Return on Assets (ROA) remaining robust at 8.00% [1].

*The 29% AUM growth and 31% disbursement surge highlight Aptus's successful market penetration, while the 22% PAT growth confirms the scalability and profitability of its lending model [1].*

## Asset Quality & Risk Metrics

Maintaining a high-quality loan book remains a core priority for Aptus, and FY24 results indicate highly effective credit risk management despite rapid portfolio growth. The company successfully reduced its non-performing assets, with the GNPA ratio improving to 1.07% (down from 1.15% in FY23) and the NNPA ratio improving to 0.80% (down from 0.86%) [1]. 

These improvements are attributed to stringent credit evaluation processes, robust risk management practices, and highly effective collection strategies [1]. Notably, Aptus achieved a remarkable collection efficiency of 100.15% in Q4 FY24, underscoring the strength of its collections infrastructure [1]. The Net Interest Margin (NIM) remained healthy at 13.45%, reflecting efficient asset-liability management [1].

## Operational Expansion

Aptus significantly scaled its physical footprint in FY24 to capture growing demand in low- and middle-income segments. The company expanded its geographic presence by opening 31 new branches, bringing the total network to 262 branches across 6 states and 1 Union territory [1]. A key milestone in this expansion was the opening of its first branch in Maharashtra, signaling a strategic push into new regional markets [1].

Branch maturity plays a critical role in AUM generation. As of March 31, 2024, branches less than 1 year old averaged ₹4.47 crore in AUM; branches 1-3 years old averaged ₹22.03 crore; and branches older than 3 years averaged ₹41.63 crore [1]. This progressive increase demonstrates the company's ability to optimize operational efficiency and generate higher revenue as branches mature [1].

## Capital Structure & Adequacy

Aptus maintains a fortress balance sheet that provides substantial headroom for future growth. The company's net worth increased by 13% to ₹3,768 crores in FY24, up from ₹3,339 crores in FY23 [1]. To fund its expanding loan portfolio, borrowings rose to ₹5,200 crores from ₹3,786 crores, resulting in a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.38:1 (up from 1.14:1 in FY23) [1].

Despite the increase in leverage, capital adequacy remains exceptionally strong. The CRAR stood at 73.03% at the end of FY24, consisting almost entirely of Tier I capital (72.44%) [1]. This is vastly superior to the regulatory minimum requirements set by the NHB/RBI, ensuring the company has a solid financial foundation to absorb potential macroeconomic shocks and fund aggressive expansion without immediate equity dilution [1]. Furthermore, Aptus maintains significant liquidity, including ₹620 crores in undrawn sanctions from banks and the NHB [1].

## Customer & Market Reach

Aptus's core mission of serving low- and middle-income groups, self-employed individuals, and small business owners yielded significant customer growth in FY24. The total customer base expanded by 25% to reach 133,499 customers [1]. During the year, the company's disbursements of ₹3,127 crores facilitated affordable housing solutions for 37,535 families [1].

The company's strategy relies on understanding the unique financial circumstances of informal segment borrowers who are often underserved by traditional banks. By offering flexible repayment options, competitive interest rates, and minimal documentation requirements—supported by an average ticket size that makes housing finance accessible—Aptus has successfully entrenched itself in rural and semi-urban markets [1].

## Strategic Initiatives & Outlook

Aptus executed several key strategic moves in FY24 to future-proof its operations and sustain its growth trajectory:

* **Geographic Expansion (FY24):** Opened 31 new branches, expanding the network to 262 locations. *Quote: "During FY24, we expanded our geographic footprint by opening 31 new branches, including our first branch in Maharashtra."* [1]
* **Leadership & Talent Enhancement (FY24):** Bolstered operational support structures. *Quote: "We also focused on strengthening our middle management across key IT, credit, technical, legal, and collections functions to support our growing operations."* [1]
* **Digital Transformation (FY24):** Advanced paperless lending and analytics. *Quote: "Widespread adoption of data-driven customer-centric digital technologies lead to paperless lending processes."* [1]

Looking ahead, management's outlook focuses on enhancing productivity, managing credit costs, and maintaining a healthy mix of financial capital [1]. The company plans to further strengthen its analytics capabilities to drive better decision-making and improve customer experiences, leveraging its omni-channel lead generation and digitally enabled underwriting [1].

## Risks & Mitigation

While Aptus's performance is stellar, the rapid pace of expansion introduces inherent risks that require careful management. 

First, the 37% increase in borrowings (to ₹5,200 crores) and the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio to 1.38:1 indicate a greater reliance on external leverage to fund growth [1]. While currently manageable given the 73.03% CRAR, any systemic liquidity tightening could increase the cost of funds and pressure the 13.45% NIM [1]. Aptus mitigates this by maintaining ₹620 crores in undrawn sanctions and a healthy mix of funding sources [1].

Second, rapid geographic expansion—particularly into new states like Maharashtra—and a 25% surge in the customer base test the scalability of the company's underwriting and collection frameworks [1]. Lending to the informal, self-employed segment carries inherent income volatility risks. Aptus is proactively mitigating these operational risks by strengthening middle management across credit and collections, and by deploying advanced credit scoring models and digital platforms to ensure underwriting discipline is not compromised as volume scales [1].
