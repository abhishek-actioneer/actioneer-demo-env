# Home First FY26 Q4 Performance Dashboard: Growth, Profitability, and Capital Strength

## Executive Summary

Home First Finance Company India Limited delivered a robust performance in Q4 FY26, characterized by a 25% year-over-year AUM expansion, record profitability, and a significantly fortified capital base [1]. The company successfully navigated a dynamic macroeconomic environment, achieving an AUM of ₹15,878 Crore and a Profit After Tax (PAT) of ₹540 Crore for the full fiscal year [1]. 

The most critical strategic development is the recent ₹1,250 Crore equity fund raise in April 2026, which propelled the Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) to an exceptional 44.1% [1]. While this provides a massive runway for future origination and geographic expansion, it introduces near-term equity dilution challenges that management must balance against margin preservation. Asset quality remains a strong point, with Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA) improving to 1.8% [1]. Moving into FY27, the company is well-positioned to achieve its guided ~25% AUM growth, provided it can effectively deploy its excess capital while managing slight spread compressions [1].

## Financial Performance Overview

Home First's FY26 results demonstrate strong top-line growth translating effectively into bottom-line profitability. The company achieved its highest-ever quarterly disbursements of ₹1,572 Crore in Q4 FY26, driving total fiscal disbursements to ₹5,424 Crore [1].

*Key Takeaways*: The 41.4% YoY surge in PAT significantly outpaced the 24.9% growth in Total Income, highlighting strong operating leverage and cost control [1]. The massive jump in CRAR is directly attributable to the April 2026 capital raise, fundamentally altering the company's balance sheet strength [1].

## Asset Quality & Risk Management

Asset quality strengthened materially throughout the quarter, reflecting disciplined underwriting and a stabilizing credit environment [1]. Early-stage delinquencies showed pronounced improvement, with 1+ DPD dropping by 60 bps QoQ to 4.7%, and 30+ DPD improving by 50 bps QoQ to 3.2% [1]. 

Consequently, GNPA reached a highly manageable 1.8%, a 20 bps improvement from the previous quarter [1]. The company maintains a conservative provisioning stance, with an ECL provision of ₹108 Crore as of March 2026, resulting in a GNPA to total provision coverage ratio (PCR) of 44.9% [1]. Credit costs remained steady at 40 bps for the quarter, aligning perfectly with management's guidance of 30–40 bps [1].

## Capital Structure & Funding Strategy

The defining event for Home First's capital structure was the ₹1,250 Crore equity fund raise completed in April 2026 [1]. This infusion dramatically increased the company's net worth to ₹4,357 Crore (up from ₹2,521 Crore in March 2025) and pushed total CRAR to 44.1%, with Tier I capital at 43.8% [1]. 

Total borrowings stood at ₹10,590 Crore as of March 2026, supported by a robust liquidity buffer of ₹3,126 Crore [1]. The cost of borrowings improved by 10 bps QoQ to 7.9% [1]. However, the Ex-CL Spread on loans experienced a slight compression, reducing by 10 bps QoQ to 5.3% [1].

## Operational Efficiency & Cost Management

Home First demonstrated significant improvements in operational efficiency during FY26. The Cost to Income ratio for the full year improved dramatically by 330 bps to 32.5%, down from 35.8% in FY25 [1]. For Q4 FY26 specifically, the ratio stood at an even more efficient 32.0% [1]. 

To support its growth trajectory, the company expanded its workforce by 221 employees, primarily in customer-facing roles, bringing the total headcount to 1,855 [1]. Despite this headcount growth, the Opex to Asset ratio remained highly controlled at 2.7% for FY26 [1].

## Growth Initiatives & Strategic Moves

The company executed several key strategic initiatives to expand its footprint and market positioning:

* **Capital Raise**: The ₹1,250 Crore equity raise in April 2026 provides a massive war chest for future growth and insulates the balance sheet against macroeconomic shocks [1].
* **Network Expansion**: During FY26, Home First added 16 new branches and 12 touchpoints, bringing its total network to 171 branches and 373 touchpoints across 13 States and Union Territories [1].
* **ESG Integration**: Under its Green Homes initiative, the company certified 140 additional homes during Q4, bringing the cumulative count to 450 as of March 2026, reinforcing its commitment to sustainable growth [1].

## Market Position & Competitive Landscape

Home First remains deeply entrenched in the affordable housing sector, targeting first-time homebuyers in low and middle-income groups [1]. Housing loans constitute the vast majority of the portfolio, contributing 83% of the total AUM [1]. Furthermore, the Economically Weaker Section (EWS) and Lower Income Group (LIG) categories form approximately 70% of the customer base [1]. The company is actively increasing its presence in emerging affordable housing markets such as Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Madhya Pradesh, and Rajasthan [1].

## Risks & Mitigation

* **Spread Compression**: The Ex-CL spread reduced by 10 bps QoQ to 5.3% [1]. The company must actively manage its cost of funds (currently at 7.9%) and optimize its asset mix to prevent further margin erosion [1].
* **Equity Dilution**: While the recent capital raise fortifies the balance sheet, the enlarged equity base will naturally pressure Return on Equity (ROE) in the near term. Q4 FY26 ROE stood at 14%, though pre-money adjusted ROE was a stronger 17.5% [1].
* **Bounce Rates**: April 2026 bounce rates remained range-bound at 16.3% [1]. While early-stage delinquencies have improved, this metric requires continuous monitoring to preempt asset quality deterioration.

## Recommendations & Action Plan

1. **Accelerate Capital Deployment**: With a CRAR of 44.1% [1], Home First is overcapitalized. Management should aggressively deploy this capital into high-yield affordable housing corridors, particularly in the newly targeted states of UP, MP, and Rajasthan, to offset the ROE dilution caused by the recent fund raise.
2. **Defend Net Interest Margins**: To combat the 10 bps QoQ spread compression [1], the company should leverage its improved credit rating and enlarged equity base to negotiate lower borrowing costs, aiming to push the cost of borrowings below the current 7.9% [1].
3. **Sustain Operational Leverage**: Having achieved a Cost to Income ratio of 32.0% in Q4 [1], the company should deepen its technology integration to ensure that the addition of 16 new branches [1] does not reverse these efficiency gains.
4. **Maintain Asset Quality Rigor**: Continue the disciplined underwriting that brought GNPA down to 1.8% [1]. Set internal KPIs to maintain 1+ DPD strictly below 5.0% (currently 4.7%) as the loan book scales toward the ~25% YoY growth target for FY27 [1].
