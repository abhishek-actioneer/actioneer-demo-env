# Home First FY 25 Competitive Snapshot: Growth Drivers, Profitability Upside & Capital-Strength Risks

## Executive Summary

Home First Finance Company India Ltd. delivered a robust financial and operational performance in FY25, characterized by significant portfolio expansion and sustained profitability. Assets Under Management (AUM) surged by 31.1% year-over-year to reach ₹12,712.72 Crores, driven by a 21.2% increase in annual disbursements to ₹4,805 Crores [1]. Despite this rapid growth, the company maintained stable asset quality, with Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA) holding steady at 1.7% and Net Non-Performing Assets (NNPA) at 1.3% [1]. 

Profitability metrics remained exceptionally strong, with Profit After Tax (PAT) growing 25.0% to ₹382.07 Crores, yielding a Return on Equity (RoE) of 16.5% [1]. The company's capital position was further fortified post-FY25 through a ₹1,250 Crore Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP) in April 2025, pushing the Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) to a highly robust 32.8% [1]. Operationally, Home First expanded its physical footprint to 155 branches and 361 touchpoints, serving an active customer base of 117,989, of which approximately 89% are women [1]. The strategic imperative moving forward is to leverage this excess capital and expanded network to capture further market share while maintaining stringent cost controls and underwriting discipline.

## Financial Performance Overview

### FY25 Revenue and Profitability Expansion

FY25 marked a period of significant top-line and bottom-line growth for Home First. Total income increased by 33.1% to ₹1,539.20 Crores, up from ₹1,156.55 Crores in FY24 [1]. This was primarily driven by a 32.0% increase in interest income on term loans, which reached ₹1,280.16 Crores [1]. Net Interest Income (NII) also saw substantial growth, reaching ₹566.79 Crores compared to ₹470.95 Crores in the previous year [1]. 

The company's ability to scale efficiently is evident in its profitability metrics. Profit After Tax (PAT) rose by 25.0% to ₹382.07 Crores [1]. While Return on Assets (RoA) saw a slight moderation to 3.5% (down from 3.8% in FY24), Return on Equity (RoE) improved by 100 basis points to 16.5% [1].

| Metric | FY24 | FY25 | YoY Growth / Variance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Total Income | ₹1,156.55 Cr | ₹1,539.20 Cr | 33.1% |
| Net Interest Income (NII) | ₹470.95 Cr | ₹566.79 Cr | 20.3% |
| Profit After Tax (PAT) | ₹305.72 Cr | ₹382.07 Cr | 25.0% |
| Return on Assets (RoA) | 3.8% | 3.5% | -30 bps |
| Return on Equity (RoE) | 15.5% | 16.5% | +100 bps |

*Key Takeaway:* Home First successfully translated its 31.1% AUM growth into a 25.0% increase in net profit, demonstrating strong operating leverage and effective margin management despite a rising cost of borrowing environment.

## Asset Quality & Risk Metrics

### Stable Portfolio Health Amidst Rapid Growth

A critical highlight of Home First's FY25 performance is the stability of its asset quality despite aggressive portfolio expansion. The company's centralized, data-science-backed underwriting process has proven resilient. Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA) remained flat at 1.7% as of March 2025, identical to the March 2024 figure [1]. Net Non-Performing Assets (NNPA) saw a marginal uptick to 1.3% from 1.2% in the previous year [1]. Credit costs were tightly controlled, remaining in line with FY24 at 30 basis points [1].

| Asset Quality Metric | Mar'24 (FY24) | Mar'25 (FY25) | Trend |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gross NPA (GNPA) | 1.7% | 1.7% | Stable |
| Net NPA (NNPA) | 1.2% | 1.3% | +10 bps |
| Credit Cost | 30 bps | 30 bps | Stable |
| Portfolio Yield | 13.5% | 13.6% | +10 bps |
| Cost of Borrowing (CoB) | 8.2% | 8.4% | +20 bps |

*Key Takeaway:* The stability in GNPA at 1.7% indicates that the 21.2% growth in disbursements was achieved without compromising underwriting standards, though the slight compression in spreads (due to a 20 bps rise in CoB) warrants monitoring [1].

## Capital Structure & Funding Strategy

### Fortified Balance Sheet Post-QIP

Home First's capital position is exceptionally strong, providing a significant runway for future growth. The Net Worth of the company increased to ₹2,521.28 Crores as of March 31, 2025, up from ₹2,121.49 Crores in FY24 [1]. The Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) stood at a robust 32.8% at the end of FY25 [1]. 

In April 2025, the company successfully completed a maiden Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP), raising ₹1,250 Crores from marquee institutional investors [1]. During FY25, the company also collaborated with 35 lenders to raise ₹5,371 Crores through various modes, ensuring diversified and stable funding [1].

| Capital & Funding Metric | FY24 | FY25 | Strategic Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Net Worth | ₹2,121.49 Cr | ₹2,521.28 Cr | Enhanced capacity to absorb risk and fund expansion. |
| CRAR | Not specified | 32.8% | Massive capital buffer well above regulatory requirements. |
| Debt to Equity Ratio | 3.4 | 3.8 | Slight increase in leverage, but remains conservative. |
| Total Funds Raised | - | ₹5,371 Cr | Demonstrates strong lender confidence and diversified access. |

*Key Takeaway:* The ₹1,250 Crore QIP significantly reduces leverage and provides Home First with a formidable capital advantage to aggressively pursue market share in its focus states [1].

## Operational Scale & Efficiency

### Network Expansion and Productivity

Home First aggressively expanded its physical footprint in FY25. The company added 75 new branches over the last three years, bringing the total branch count to 155 as of March 2025 (up from 133 in March 2024) [1]. Total touchpoints reached 361, covering 141 districts across 13 states and one union territory [1]. 

To support this expansion, the employee base grew to 1,634 from 1,249 in the previous year [1]. Despite this rapid headcount addition, productivity remained relatively stable. Disbursement per employee stood at ₹3.3 Crores in FY25, slightly down from ₹3.5 Crores in FY24, while Opex-to-Average Total Assets improved to 2.7% from 2.9% [1].

| Operational Metric | FY24 | FY25 | Growth / Variance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Physical Branches | 133 | 155 | +22 branches |
| Total Touchpoints | Not specified | 361 | Significant network density |
| Total Employees | 1,249 | 1,634 | +385 employees |
| Disbursement / Employee | ₹3.5 Cr | ₹3.3 Cr | Slight moderation due to new hires |
| Opex to Average Assets | 2.9% | 2.7% | -20 bps (Efficiency gain) |

*Key Takeaway:* The improvement in the Opex-to-Assets ratio to 2.7% demonstrates that Home First's technology-driven processes are successfully absorbing the costs of rapid branch and employee expansion [1].

## Market Reach & Customer Base

### Deepening Penetration in Focus Markets

Home First's customer base grew by 24% to reach 117,989 active customers by the end of FY25 [1]. The company maintains a strong focus on financial inclusion and the aspiring middle class. Notably, approximately 89% of the borrowers in FY25 were women, and loans provided to Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) and Low-Income Groups (LIG) accounted for 61% of the total AUM [1]. 

Geographically, the company continues to concentrate on its core markets. As of March 2025, 114 of its branches were located in the western and southern states of India (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana), which the company identifies as its focus states [1].

## Strategic Initiatives & Growth Drivers

### Key Actions Shaping FY25 and Beyond

Home First executed several strategic moves in FY25 to solidify its market position and diversify its revenue streams.

* **Capital Raise (Apr 2025):** Successfully completed a maiden QIP, raising ₹1,250 Crores to strengthen the balance sheet and reduce leverage [1].
* **Geographic Expansion (FY25):** Expanded reach into 10 new districts and added 22 new physical branches, bringing the total to 155 branches across 141 districts [1].
* **Product Diversification (Aug 2024):** Commenced corporate agency business and started accruing insurance income, which drove a 35.5% growth in non-interest income (Fee and Commission income rose to ₹45.3 Crores in FY25 from ₹9.9 Crores in FY24) [1].

## Risks & Mitigation

### Navigating Growth and Liquidity Pressures

While the growth trajectory is strong, several risks require active management. The rapid expansion of the branch network (adding 75 branches in three years) and employee base introduces operational and execution risks [1]. If new branches do not scale to profitability quickly, the currently excellent Opex-to-Assets ratio (2.7%) could face upward pressure [1]. 

Furthermore, FY25 witnessed a tight liquidity position in the banking system, which caused the company's Cost of Borrowing (CoB) to increase by 20 bps to 8.4% [1]. While portfolio yields were maintained at 13.6%, overall spreads compressed slightly to 5.2% (excluding co-lending) from 5.5% in FY24 [1]. The company mitigates this through a diversified borrowing mix, having raised funds from 35 different lenders [1].
